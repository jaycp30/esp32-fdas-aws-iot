How one zone input works

  1. Dry contact. The panel's zone relay closes a plain switch across J1 pin 1 (signal) and pin 2 (field supply +). The panel supplies no voltage, only the contact.
  2. Field loop. About 21 mA flows from the 24 V field supply through R6 (1 k), the red LED1 and the PC817's internal LED, then back to the field 0 V.
  3. Isolation. Light crosses the gap inside the PC817. No copper crosses the routed slot between the field side and the logic side.
  4. Collector goes low. The PC817's transistor saturates and pulls its collector from 3.3 V down to about 0.1 V against R11, the 10 k pull-up. Meter A shows it.
  5. Inverted to HIGH. The 74HC04 flips that, so input-only GPIO36 is driven to 3.3 V (Meter B). Energized means HIGH, and the pin never floats.
  6. Firmware. It polls the pin instead of using interrupts, because GPIO36 and 39 can glitch while Wi-Fi is on. Zone ON after 50 ms; OFF only after 1 s open.
  7. Status LED. Every 500 ms: red while any input is active, otherwise green on Wi-Fi, or blinking blue (1 Hz) without it.

Caveat: the slot only isolates in 24 V mode with a separately powered board. In 5 V mode, or when the board is fed by a non-isolated buck from the panel's own 24 V, both sides share a ground.

Key

  • Relay armature and contact, meter needles: what moves when the zone changes
  • Copper: a net drawn bright is at 3.3 V; the dashed field loop means current flows
  • Routed slot: field side and logic side share no copper
  • RGB LED red: an input is active
  • Green: idle, Wi-Fi up
  • Blinking blue: Wi-Fi down
